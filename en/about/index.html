About this site

The companion to Quantum Computing — Part I: Circuits, Algorithms and Codes.

What is here

14 lectures with 24 definitions, 63 numbered results, 56 proofs and 30 worked examples. The notes, numbered objects and circuit diagrams come from the same sources as the PDF. You can choose the Lithuanian or English edition.

Part I is fourteen lectures in three blocks: circuits and the model of computation; the differences between quantum and classical computation; and protection against noise. Part II is reserved for later, continuing the numbering from 15.

How much is proved

A result with no tag is proved in full. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used with its proof in the references. Every complexity claim also says whether it is proved, holds relative to an oracle, or is conjectured.

Checking your understanding

Each lecture ends with self-check questions. A first wrong answer gives a hint; after a second, you see the explanation. A correct first-attempt answer, given without a hint, counts as independent.

You can tick learning goals yourself and follow the adjacent links to the relevant sections. These ticks are your own assessment. Separate lecture exercise sheets are not available yet. Problems also links a worked-practice PDF with hints and complete solutions.

Your data

No account or login is needed. Your display preferences, self-check answers, learning-goal ticks and annotations are saved in this browser profile. The optional learning profile is managed on My progress, where you can download a backup, import one or remove local learning records.

Progress does not synchronise automatically with another device. The site does not send your learning records to a server. Opening an external AI tool takes you to that service's website.

Found a mistake?

Keep a written erratum for the author: include the page URL or PDF edition, lecture and result number or section, the quoted passage, and, if possible, a calculation or counterexample and proposed correction. You can record it in a local text file and share it with the author through any contact channel you already use. Attendance at a lecture or tutorial is not needed.
